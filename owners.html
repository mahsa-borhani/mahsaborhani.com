Across my career

Running it as if I owned it

Owners have stepped back and left the business running in my hands. That trust isn’t luck: it rests on a system.

The situation

At the décor brand, about 14 months in, the founders moved their attention to their other businesses and left this one with me; after that we met about every two months. My manager there wasn’t from e-commerce or technology and, after about three months, gave me the function outright: monthly reporting, decisions mine.

In my current role, the owner stepped back from the start.

What I did

  • Built the system first, so the business doesn’t depend on me being in the room. The quotation desk, the AI agents and the reporting all run without me.
  • Kept a reporting rhythm where the owner always knows what I know.
  • Pushed back early, with the number behind it, the way an owner would.
  • Grew into it deliberately, including regular sessions with a business coach for more than a year.

The result

By my fifth month in the current role I was deciding most business matters, and net margin is up 19 points. What owners tell me is that I run the business as if I own it.

What I learned

For an owner, stepping back shouldn’t be a leap of faith. It’s just what the numbers let you do.

Skills in play

  • Ownership
  • Reporting rhythm
  • Systems design
  • Executive communication
