The 73% drop that wasn’t

Timing, not ads

Our pre-buy ahead of a supplier price rise worked so well that August read 73% below July. Before anyone raised ad spend, I showed the drop was timing, not a collapse, and we kept the budget where it was.

73%apparent drop, July to August
12 daysahead, I had written down that August would look softer

The situation

Our supplier raised prices on August 1, and we helped customers buy ahead. Three large orders landed in the last eleven days of July. Then August revenue came in far below July, and the obvious reaction would have been more ad spend.

What I did

  • I had already written down, twelve days ahead, that August would look softer because of the pre-buy.
  • Took the number apart: the order count barely moved from July. Order size fell, because big buyers had bought early.
  • Checked the other suspect: ad spend had barely moved, so it was arithmetically incapable of causing a gap that size.

The result

We kept the budget where it was instead of spending into a problem that did not exist.

What I learned

Every big sale pulls demand forward. I plan the month after before the sale runs.

Skills in play

  • Diagnosis
  • Forecasting
  • Promotions and seasonality
  • Budget decisions

How it’s measured

July against August 2026, paid orders, excluding our largest account and internal orders.
