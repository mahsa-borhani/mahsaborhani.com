Building-products business · 2025–now

Net margin up 19 points

+19 pts

A B2B building-products business selling across Canada and the US, with a full P&L reporting to ownership. I rebuilt the measurement, put money only behind what proved it paid, and rebuilt how quotations are made. Net margin is up 19 points.

The commercial problem

When I joined, nobody could say what the advertising money bought: the account was optimised against conversion tracking that had silently stopped recording sales. And more than 90% of revenue came through the quotation desk, which an online-checkout metric never sees.

What I did

  • Rebuilt the measurement layer before spending: GA4, Merchant Center, conversion imports, UTM architecture, and feed and pixel integrity.
  • Put budget back deliberately, only behind campaigns that proved they paid, and moved money to what brought the most value: an influencer partnership and trade shows.
  • Ran the ad platforms on a cadence, not set-and-forget: negative keywords, expansions switched off where they didn’t pay, clean conversion tags and a clean product feed, with regular sessions with Google’s Ads specialists.
  • Rebuilt the quotation desk, so nine in ten enquiries get a first draft quotation within two hours.
  • Rebuilt the pricing architecture into six B2B pricing catalogues by customer tier, with separate go-to-market plans for the US and Canada and automated follow-up.
  • Brought operating costs down 37%, about 70% of it from online advertising.

The result

Net margin up 19 points, with operating costs down 37%; gross profit now covers operating costs about 1.6×. Revenue grew 1.71× on 28% fewer orders as the average order grew 2.4×, and by early September the core business had passed 130% of the prior year’s revenue with almost four months to go.

Revenue grew on 88% less ad spend: revenue paid through the website more than doubled (+135%, January to April), led by moving quotation invoices to online checkout. The best single campaign, a US Performance Max, reported a 25.6× return on ad spend over 30 days; I report that as platform-attributed, because incremental lift was not measured and later windows read about 6×.

And the momentum built: each 30-day sprint beat the same dates a year earlier by more, 1.96×, then 2.06×, then 2.40×.

What I learned

Money follows proof.

And one I use often: a busier market brings more orders. It doesn’t bring bigger ones.

Skills in play

How it’s measured

From the business’s own accounts. Net margin compares the twelve months before I joined with my first months in the role; profit figures are all-in. Revenue compares January 1 to September 3 with the same days a year earlier, with the largest account excluded in both years. Gross margin did not improve: the gain came from operating costs and larger orders. One-time items are kept out of every figure.
