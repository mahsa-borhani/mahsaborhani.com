Every decision rests on a number. I make sure the numbers are real before anyone decides on them, and that anything a customer sees about price or specifications is right.

44% of the catalogue, offline

Hundreds of products had stopped serving in Shopping, free listings and Performance Max while every dashboard looked normal. I traced all of them to one deliberate setting: a quotation-only shipping service with a placeholder price and “please call” in its name. Correct on the storefront for pallet freight; unreadable to Merchant Center, which took it literally.

Traffic that wasn’t customers

A spike of 28× normal in a week, 71× on the peak day, came from a bot network with an impossible square screen resolution. I shipped a reusable filter, so every conversion rate the team reads is real.

Enquiries no metric could see

Most revenue arrives by phone and quotation, which an online-checkout metric never sees. In September 2026 I built the Google Tag Manager event layer: click and form listeners wired to four Google Ads conversion actions, with the conversion linker in place.

Protecting what customers see

A routine price clean-up would have raised two contracted customers’ prices by about 14% as a side effect; I checked how their price lists were linked and stopped it before anything changed. When the product behind 83% of 90-day revenue showed the wrong price on most sizes, I fixed it in one reversible change and checked every draft order: no customer had been charged the wrong price. And I caught that the same insulation is labelled R22 in Canada and R23 in the US before a US sponsor quotation went out.

The strategy I retired

I backed five pages built to one template. After 90 days, two carried the whole programme and three earned almost nothing. The results tracked what people actually search for, not the page format, so I said so openly and retired the approach I had backed.

What I learned

Dashboards aggregate, and exceptions hide inside the totals.

And: look for the comparison you already have before building more of the same.
