Building-products business · 2025–now

They didn’t need boards. They needed kits.

1.66×

Our largest account is a US manufacturer of prefabricated data-centre modules: a customer like our others, who found us online before I joined. I’ve run it end to end since, and grew it 1.66× in my first year.

The commercial problem

The account had gone six months without a new order, and our reporting flagged it as at risk.

What I did

  • Checked the relationship instead of the flag: they were between projects.
  • Listening to how they actually built their modules, I moved them from loose insulation board to factory-cut kits built to their module drawings, on custom pallets.
  • Rebuilt the pricing for palletized loads (five units a container instead of six) and the full delivered cost from the factory to their plant, then proposed a review with their buyer and engineers.
  • Held price until the specifications were clear.
  • Run the cross-border container supply chain from an overseas factory to their US plant: about nine weeks from factory to site, with per-order invoicing.
  • Built the sales kit for the segment (a tiered lead roster, a technical sales sheet and wall-assembly guide, a competitor battlecard and a build map of 56 sites), then re-based the strategy on order data, which showed growth came from expanding existing kit-buying accounts, not cold prospecting.

The result

Three weeks after the review they named two projects. The first orders, nine orders across eight containers, were placed in a single week in June, ahead of a supplier price increase, with more containers scheduled into 2027. The account grew 1.66× in my first year.

What I learned

Lifetime value is built after the first order: the next project, the add-ons, the referral.
