05 · How I work

Six habits behind the results

The same few habits show up in every chapter, from a décor brand’s storefronts to a distributor’s branches.

Measure before spending

Money follows proof.

Build the system first

The quotation desk, the AI agents and the reporting all run without me.

Make people co-owners

Not subjects. That’s how a whole company adopts something new.

Test my own ideas hardest

Look for the comparison you already have before building more of the same.

Trust the order table

Over the dashboard. Dashboards aggregate, and exceptions hide inside the totals.

Keep brakes on the AI

Anything irreversible waits for a person.

06 · AI in production

AI agents, with brakes

I built a team of AI agents myself, in Claude Code: about twenty roles across sales, operations, reporting, advertising and QA. It works on live store, advertising, search and CRM data every day: monitoring, anomaly alerts, reporting, forecasting and research. Twenty-two versioned releases since April 2026, and I’m now coordinating AI across two companies in the group.

  1. The agent that produces never reviewsA different agent checks the work.
  2. Outside claims need two sourcesNothing customer-facing rests on one source.
  3. Anything irreversible waits for a personSpending, sending and deleting stay human decisions.
  4. Every change is versionedReleases live in Git, so any change can be traced and undone.
  • The review is real. QA sends back four in ten first drafts. A gate that never fails anything is decoration.
  • The brakes hold. An automated finding once proposed cancelling five container orders as duplicates. The approval rule held, and all five were real.
  • Least privilege. Its Microsoft 365 access reaches one SharePoint site and nothing else, approved by IT as written.
  • People use it. A plain-English guide, role playbooks and safety rules, and six colleagues coached one-on-one.
