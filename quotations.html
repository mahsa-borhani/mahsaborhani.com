Building-products business · 2025–now

Quotations in hours, not days

9 in 10

More than 90% of revenue comes through the quotation desk, and a quotation order is worth about 14 online orders. So I designed a quotation engine around one idea: high-value buyers want a fast, exact answer.

The commercial problem

Enquiries used to land with a colleague or in the shared sales inbox, and wait on purchasing, vendors and freight costs before anyone could reply. That took one to four days.

Checking requests one by one against the mailbox, after the platform’s own “unquoted” flag turned out to answer a different question, I found more than a third still waiting for a reply. When replies did happen they came within hours: the business had an answering problem, not a conversion problem.

What I did

  • Found the bottleneck, then designed the workflow and the rules.
  • Brought in a developer to build a custom quotation and CRM lifecycle platform to my design, integrating ERP, PIM and CRM so the whole lifecycle runs in one place.
  • In one breath, what it does: a customer asks in bags, square feet, bundles, sheets or pallets; it works out the quantities, the pallets and a freight estimate, picks the in-stock location that is most affordable for the customer, checks the landed-cost margin and warns before a price falls below break-even, and produces the quotation and invoice, ready to send or held for approval.
  • Its AI keeps each customer’s history, drafts replies, answers routine questions and follows up when a buyer goes quiet. Anything I’ve marked for approval waits for a person.
  • Delivered the mailbox finding with a named owner, a one-business-day first-response target and a decision date.
  • Built a 700+ contact CRM with automated onboarding, follow-up and win-back journeys, scored by a lead-scoring rubric I designed (open +5, click +15, reply +30, quotation +50, order +100), on a near-zero monthly tool cost.
  • Wrote a follow-up email to ageing quotations; the platform’s own July report credits me with influenced revenue.

The result

If a person is on the system, a quotation and a written answer reach the customer in under two hours; in the past it was one to four days. Nine in ten enquiries get a first draft quotation within two hours, typically within minutes: a real answer, not an acknowledgement.

The quotation win rate rose from 24% to 33% to 44% across the first three quarters of 2026, by quarter the quotation was created. The share of customer requests with a linked quotation doubled from 33% to 66% between the second and third quarters, and won value grew 34% in US dollars and 61% in Canadian dollars over the same quarters.

In July alone the margin check raised 34 warnings before prices fell below break-even, and in its first two months the platform answered more than 200 reporting questions. Measuring the desk by order source also changed how website spend is judged: on whether it produces a request for quotation, not a cart.

What I learned

High-value buyers want a fast, exact answer.

Skills in play

  • Quotation-to-cash
  • Process design
  • CRM and lifecycle
  • ERP, PIM and CRM integration
  • Margin control
  • Lead scoring

How it’s measured

The two-hour figure is measured in the quotation platform, May to October 2026. The one to four days is what I watched in the shared inbox before the system.
